Jensen Huang, Our Man of the Year: Greatness Comes With Consequences

He helped change the world. He also let people down. His resignation gives us a harder conversation about achievement, accountability and the limits of a founder’s claim on his company.

A man can build something extraordinary and still fail the people closest to him. Holding those two facts together is uncomfortable. It is also where an honest conversation about leadership has to begin.

Jensen Huang is our Man of the Year because his story now forces that conversation. The builder who became one of the defining figures of the artificial-intelligence era has stepped away from the company he co-founded. His achievements remain. So do the consequences of his choices. Neither cancels the other.

The achievement is real

NVIDIA’s biography dates Huang’s founding of the company to 1993. Across the decades that followed, graphics processors became central to a much wider computing transformation. His career helped put accelerated computing at the heart of an industry that now reaches far beyond gaming.

That is an extraordinary professional legacy. It is also an insufficient answer to a question about conduct. A company’s importance does not make its founder exempt from obligations to family, colleagues or the institution he helped create.

So is the failure

In a November 8 message published by NVIDIA, Huang acknowledged being unfaithful to his wife, apologised to his family and colleagues, and accepted responsibility for the harm he had caused. He also said he supported the board’s independent investigation into national-security concerns raised in reporting.

The admission matters. An apology that names the act and the people hurt gives accountability somewhere to begin. It does not finish the work. Trust belongs to the people whose trust was damaged; it cannot be restored by the applause of strangers.

The company has to be bigger than one man

On November 12, NVIDIA announced Huang’s resignation as president, chief executive and director. Colette Kress was appointed interim chief executive, with Harvey C. Jones becoming chair of the board. The company began a search for a permanent successor.

For us, the lesson is the importance of allowing an institution to continue beyond the person most closely associated with it. Founding a company creates a profound relationship. It does not create an unlimited entitlement to lead it. Employees, customers and shareholders have lives and obligations beyond the founder’s story.

The public announcement establishes the transition. It cannot tell us every conversation behind closed doors. We should therefore be careful about turning a resignation into proof of private serenity or claiming that there was no conflict with the board. What we can assess is the public acknowledgement of wrongdoing and the fact that the leadership changed.

What we mean by man of the year

This is an editorial choice, not a declaration of moral perfection. We are interested in a man whose scale of achievement and scale of consequence make the questions impossible to avoid. Did he fail? By his own account, yes. Does acknowledging that failure matter? Yes. Does stepping down erase it? No.

The standard should travel. We would ask the same of Elon Musk, a founder running a small business or a man leading his household: can you hear that you have caused harm without making your status the centre of the conversation? Can you accept a consequence you would rather avoid? The test becomes more demanding as power grows.

There is room to recognise the builder without asking the people hurt by his behaviour to become supporting characters in a redemption story. Their forgiveness is not ours to award. Nor should allegations still under investigation be treated as established findings.

An ending we cannot write yet

Huang may return to a prominent leadership role, or he may not. We do not know. A comeback would not by itself establish repair, just as an absence from the spotlight would not tell us everything about the work being done in private.

What makes this moment worth examining is the boundary it puts around greatness. Talent does not settle every argument. Building an institution does not place a man above it. The ability to change the world must live alongside the willingness to answer for what you do inside it.
