Finding Faith When Life Comes Apart

An editorial reflection on asking for help and beginning again.

MENCLAVE · EDITORIAL CONCEPT

Fictional first-person story

This narrative is an original fiction written to demonstrate a longer personal essay with two photographs. It is not a reader testimony.

The first morning after the job ended, I still woke at six. I made coffee, dressed and opened my laptop. There was no meeting to join. The silence felt less like freedom than a question I had spent years avoiding.

The answer I kept giving

For the first week I said I was taking some time out. It was a useful sentence: tidy, voluntary, almost enviable. My friend did not challenge it. He just kept asking whether I wanted to walk on Thursday.

On the third Thursday I told him I was embarrassed. Not simply worried about bills, though I was. Embarrassed that I could no longer introduce myself through a job title. We kept walking. He did not offer a quotation about closed doors.

Something smaller than certainty

I expected faith to arrive as an explanation. Instead, what helped was a routine: breakfast before emails, a walk before another application, an honest conversation before another polished update. None of these actions solved the uncertainty. They made it possible to live inside it.

I did not need a better announcement. I needed a more honest day.

I began writing down what I could do that afternoon. Some days it was an application. Some days it was asking a former colleague for feedback. On the days when shame made everything heavy, it was answering the friend who had kept checking in.

An unfinished ending

There is no triumphant final paragraph in the notebook from that month. The next job had not appeared. What had changed was the list of people who knew the truth. I had stopped making uncertainty a private performance.

If I could speak to the version of myself at that first empty desk, I would not promise a quick turnaround. I would tell him to eat breakfast, pick up the phone and let someone walk beside him before he knew where the road would lead.
